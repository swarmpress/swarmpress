# ADR-0030 — Personas are a data catalog with CV, hobbies and interests

**Status:** Accepted
**Date:** 2026-10-01

## Context

Every member of staff and every hiring candidate should feel like a real person: a CV,
education, past jobs, hobbies, interests, quirks, likes, dislikes and relationships. That detail
does three jobs:
- it makes meeting transcripts characterful;
- it fills the profile cards and the hiring UI;
- it drives what people are good at.

The legacy schema had a `hobbies` column that nobody ever populated, and personas existed only
for writers.

## Decision

- Personas are **data**, one TOML file per person in `crates/agents/personas/`. The schema is
  in docs/game-design/organization.md §3: identity (name, stated pronouns, age, hometown,
  languages), department, role, title, seniority, asking salary, bio, `[cv]`, `[life]`,
  `[traits]`, optional `[writing_style]`, `[relationships]`, `[appearance]`, and topic
  affinities.
- The **deterministic sim stores only** `PersonaId` plus the numbers it needs (traits,
  seniority, salary). Text never enters the sim or its hash.
- The same files feed:
  - prompts: `agents::personas` formatters;
  - the UI: bundled into the client and served by the server;
  - the hiring pool: unemployed personas.
- New candidates are generated by an LLM job (`CandidateGeneration`) against the same schema.
  They are validated, and are rejected if they duplicate a name or are incomplete, before they
  enter the pool.
- Pronouns are explicit fields and are never inferred from names.

## Consequences

- Writing personas becomes content work. Each needs a reviewable, realistic CV.
- The catalog grows with play: generated candidates are stored per company on the server, while
  the repo holds the seed catalog.
- Trait numbers stay authoritative for the sim, even if a CV "sounds" more senior. The prompt
  formatter shows both, so voices stay consistent with sim behaviour.
